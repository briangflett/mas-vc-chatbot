ABOUT THIS HANDBOOK

This handbook is an introduction to MAS for new Volunteer Consultants (VCs) and a reference for current VCs. It does not replace ongoing participation for up-to-date information about MAS and how we operate.

MAS processes continue to change over time as we find better ways to operate and to serve non-profit clients.  This handbook captures our current organization and processes; it will be revised from time to time.  

This handbook reviews how MAS does business.  It does not attempt to teach VCs how to be effective consultants.

Our History

MAS was formed in 1993 by a few volunteers with a good idea.  The initial Volunteer Consultants came largely from the CESO (Canadian Executive Service Organization) roster of volunteers.  MAS was associated with the Volunteer Center of Toronto until 2004 when it became an independent corporation. 

In 2005 MAS was granted charitable status. In 2007, after a transition period, all VCs were invited to become members of MAS and a 10 member Board was elected.  

In 2008, the Board established an Executive Director position, filled by three volunteers (Sue Croft, Steve Gittins, and Sue Pulfer).  In 2022 our first paid (part time) Executive Director was hired, Paddy Bowen.

In 2009 we received a grant from Trillium to allow us to pay someone to administer the office, a part time Program Co-coordinator.  Nina Rahenbrock currently provides this service. 

In 2018 MAS celebrated its 25th year of offering pro bono services to charities and not for profits.  We have continued to increase the number of projects our Volunteer Consultants undertake for clients – for example in 2019 we received over 191 consulting assignments. 

What We Do

MAS attracts volunteers with extensive business/professional experience to provide management consulting services to non-profit clients.  Non-profit status is the only qualification we have for clients subject to them having the ability to take advantage of consulting assistance. We match our clients to skilled professional Volunteer Consultants. Services are delivered primarily to clients in the GTA however we may seek to expand our geographic area in future. 

MAS VCs must have the relevant skills and experience and an ability to forge strong relationships. At the core of the client relationships is trust.  A VC must build trust with the client, get a clear understanding of the client’s situation, and assist the client in identifying solutions they can implement.

Consultants undertake consulting assignments with specific deliverables.  VCs form a variety of relationships with their clients. Some consultants form longer-term relationships with clients and provide ongoing coaching or ad hoc consulting services (also defined projects).   VCs are encouraged to keep in touch with previous clients and to solicit further projects for MAS.

We have defined our consulting practices as below, to best match VCs to clients. However, if a client requests assistance that falls outside a practice area we will refer them to the most appropriate VC, if they have the expertise to do the assignment - whether or not it fits neatly in our categories.

Exceptions

There are a few areas where MAS will decline to provide services.  We do not provide:

any service that requires a licensed professional (audit, legal advice, etc.)  

hands-on IT support (although we do IT consulting)

temporary employees or fill interim positions.

With these exceptions, we try our best to meet any request for consulting services keeping the MAS Mission always in mind:  MAS helps Not for Profit organizations and sub-sectors to develop and grow, especially those most in need, through the targeted use of MAS capabilities and expertise.

Project Life Cycle (Appendix A)

Our basic project management process involves three forms that capture the agreement with the client at each stage of the project: 

Client Engagement 

Project Execution

Project Close 

Client Engagement (see Request for Consulting Services form p.9 )

Projects generally originate when a non-profit makes contact with the MAS office or with a VC they know or have been referred to by an outside party (e.g. a previous client).  Clients can contact the office via phone, email, website or regular mail.  The office responds to all enquiries, gathers initial information and asks the client to complete the Request for Consulting Services form.  

Once the request is fleshed out with all available information it is circulated by email to the group of VCs that are assigned to the practice area best suited to deal with it. VCs may then indicate an interest by notifying the MAS office.  The MAS office then puts the client and VC in touch with one another.  

If the request is unclear or there are many issues, the Client Services Manager will clarify over the telephone or, if necessary, arrange for one of our Generalists or Practice Leaders to contact the client and develop a request that is then circulated to the VCs in the relevant practice. 

The form makes clear that a volunteer consultant identified by MAS will be referred to the client. It also seeks client agreement that MAS will not be liable for any loss or damage incurred by the Organization or third parties affected by or involved in the Project as a result of the consulting/advisory services provided by the volunteer consultant referred to the Organization by MAS.

Project Execution (see Project Definition form p.9 )

When the VC accepts an assignment, the VC meets with the client, establishes a relationship and develops a Project Definition that defines the deliverables and is signed by the client and the VC.  

It is very important to the success of a project that the VC clarifies the client’s expectations, that the client and the VC understand their respective roles in the project and that the deliverables are clear. If for any reason the VC or Client decides not to proceed then the MAS office should be advised and, if called for, another VC will be assigned. 

At this stage it is important to explain to the client, especially new clients, who MAS is and how we operate, including the fact that we are financed largely by client donations and that the organization will ask the client to consider a donation at the close of the project. (This “ask” may be done by the Volunteer Consultant or by the Executive Director).  However, whether or not a client makes a donation has no impact on our response to future requests for service.

Most projects occur through a series of meetings over a couple of months.  Sometimes there are significant delays in the work due to client capacity or timing and on occasion projects can be protracted. In these cases, the VC is responsible for ensuring that the client still feels there is a priority for the project. If the client is not able to support the project, it can be put on hold until the client can support it. Projects should not be put on hold for longer than 6 months. When a project has been on hold for 6 months the client services manager will review the project with the VC to see if it should be closed, cancelled or left on hold.  If necessary, the project can be cancelled.

The deliverables and how they are accomplished vary by the type of assignment.  Meetings may take place at the client’s place of business, by telephone or by videoconference and the VC also does work at home (e.g., meeting preparation, documentation, report writing).  A VC may work alone or ask for help from other VCs. VCs are encouraged to work together, as appropriate. This promotes learning and development as well as shares the workload.

Generally, we undertake only one project at a time for a client.  Most of our clients are small and cannot handle multiple projects simultaneously.  In cases involving several issues (e.g. governance, financial management, planning) we will work with the client to establish a priority and do one project at a time. However, if we do agree to a second project while another is still open a Project Definition will be required for the second project but not an RCS.

Project Close  

When the project has been completed the VC has a discussion with the client to review the original Project Definition and discuss the work completed and outcomes delivered.  This is an excellent time to ask for feedback from the client; 

what went well

what did we learn

are there opportunities for future projects between MAS and the client

IF comfortable and it seems appropriate, mention the possibility of a donation (NOTE: the MAS office will include this ask in closing documentation as well)

     The VC advises the client that they will be receiving a Project Close email from MAS and  

     requests that they complete the information and return to MAS.

      Many VCs follow up this final conversation with a “thank you” email.

Request for Consulting Services [RCS]

This is the basic consulting agreement. It should be completed by a new client prior to any service provision and sent to the MAS office.  If we have dealt with the client previously and have a fairly recent (2 years) form on file, it is not necessary to complete another one though often they do which helps define the current project.

If the client contact person has changed then the original RCS should be shared with the new client contact.

Project Definition

This is the agreement between the VC and the client on the specific deliverables for the project. It should be completed at the start of the project with the client and sent to the MAS office.

Reporting: Monthly Status Reports

Each month the VC is required to provide a brief (sentence or two) update on activities in the past month and expected activities in the next month for every open project.  The Client Services Manager will email the VCs a link to a web form used to provide these updates.  This information is critical to managing the active projects.

Project Close

When the work has been completed, the VC completes a Project Close form.  The form is forwarded to the MAS office who then forwards it to the client. The client is invited to add their comments to the Project Close form and return to the MAS office, however, this is not required. 

The following are detailed instructions on how to complete the Project Close form:

VC - Instructions for completing the form

There are two parts to the online form – one for the VC and one for the client.

Click on your saved link (see above) to go to the project close page on the MAS website

Click on the link to open the form

Fill in all the fields in the top section (called the Volunteer Consultant section)

Scroll down past the Client section to the bottom of the form and click on “VC save and continue later”

Draft and send an email to the Client Services Manager (info@masadvise.org) requesting that she closes the project. Paste the link (from your clipboard) into the email.

What happens then...

The Client Services Manager sends the link to the client with a personalized note asking them to complete the MAS Project Close form using the link and thanking them for working with MAS etc.  The Client Services Manager will then close the project in the database.

The client clicks the link and fills out the client portion of the form and clicks on the “submit” button to submit it.

The completed form will then reside on our WordPress database allowing the Client Services Manager to access it and run reports.

If you feel it is inappropriate to ask the client to fill in the client portion (project cancelled, ED resigned etc.), then complete the VC portion and send the link to the Client Services Manager telling her the reason. They will put the reason in the client portion and submit it when they close the project. 

When a project closes, the VC may choose to encourage the client to consider a donation to MAS but there is no requirement to do so.  The MAS office will ask for a donation during the administrative closing process.  In our experience most clients will willingly make a contribution if they have the funds.  Our story as a volunteer organization with minimal expenses supported largely by our volunteers and our clients is a powerful one. 

Notes:

The Project Close form captures the hours spent by VCs on a project.  VCs must track these hours.  If more than one VC is involved in the project then hours for each VC should be recorded. This information is critical to MAS in making our case about the value we provide to the community.

These forms are the required paperwork for every project undertaken by MAS.  The forms must be sent to the MAS office promptly.  Every VC should be familiar with these forms. As the forms are revised from time to time new versions are posted to the VC Support Centre on the internal web page. 

If the Volunteer Consultant has elected to ask the client for a donation they must alert the Client Services Manager so we don’t duplicate the request unknowingly during the closing process.

Donations from Clients

Donations from our clients have grown steadily over the last few years and we need to ensure this continues. VCs play an important part in this. The fact that we accept donations is displayed prominently on all standard MAS forms that our clients review, plus on all our promotional material. Therefore, they know we are looking for donations. 

In the initial meeting with a client the VC should inform them that, while we do not charge fees, we do accept donations to cover our office expenses. 

At the end of the project, the subject of donations will again be raised. As noted above this may be done by the VC if it makes sense and/or a request for a donation will be made by the Executive Director as part of the closing process.

An example of what might be inserted in that email is as follows:

“As you know, MAS is financially supported by our volunteers and donations from our clients. If you were satisfied with the results of this assignment, we would appreciate it if you would consider a donation to help us keep the office running”

In the instance where a VC raises the possibility of a donation with the client, the Client Services Manager and/or ED should be alerted so the ‘request for a donation’ letter can be adjusted accordingly.

How much to ask for

You may be asked to suggest the size of a donation. The following notes, expressed in your own words, are what you might say in response to such a request.

“MAS receives a wide range of donations mostly from $100 to $1,000 with an average of about $500 and with some as high as $5,000.  Organizations usually set the amount in keeping with their budget and the consulting time spent on the project and their degree of satisfaction with the outcomes.” 


Obviously, many agencies cannot afford to donate and in that case we don’t expect any donation at all.

When a new VC joins MAS, they are assigned to specific practice areas (e.g. facilitation, finance, strategic planning).  The normal process is for new client requests to be circulated via email to the VCs in the relevant practice and each of those VCs has the opportunity to accept the assignment. 

Our Structure

MAS is an incorporated entity and has charitable status.  We are a membership corporation with an elected Board of Directors.  The organization chart and contact information is attached as Appendix G.

MAS members may attend the Annual General Meeting and vote on matters affecting the corporation, including election of Board members. All VCs are encouraged to become members and take part in the corporate business, but corporate membership is not a requirement.  Currently, anyone who makes at least a $50 donation to MAS and is approved by the Board is made a member through to the following AGM. 

The Executive Director role is responsible to the Board for the operations and management of MAS.  A paid part time individual currently fills this role which until 2022 was carried out by volunteers. Other volunteers fill additional roles in the management structure (see organization chart) and provide assistance in the office and work on special projects from time to time.

The Client Services Manager (CSM) is responsible for the office and is the primary initial client contact as well as the point of coordination for most routine activities e.g. receiving and dealing with client requests or consulting assistance.  

We encourage VCs to become involved in ongoing MAS work. This is an excellent way to get to know the organization, the sector and other VCs.  To volunteer for any of these areas, contact the appropriate person on the organization chart.

Financial Matters

MAS operates on a very limited budget.  Our main expense is the services of the Client Services Manager and the Executive Director which are both part time positions. Our principal funding sources are clients and our VCs and our policy is to operate as a financially self-sufficient organization, supported by agencies who benefit from our service and VC donations.

MAS Volunteer Consultant Job Description

Purpose

The role of the MAS Volunteer Consultant is to work with non-profit organizations providing consultation and guidance to help them implement improvements or changes that enhance their ability to achieve organizational mission and success.

Role

To determine, with the client and the basis of the Request for provided by them, the nature of the perceived problem and work with the client to develop solutions and strategies (work plan) to address. 

Focus

The majority of work done by MAS Volunteer Consultants is focused in the following Practice areas.  Where more than one area requires intervention, each should be constructed as a separate project following MAS processes.

Governance

Strategic Planning

Human Resources, including volunteer management and coaching

Communications, Marketing and Fundraising

Information Management and Technology

Finance

New organization start-up

Functions

Representing MAS to the client

Explain MAS to the client; our mission, who we are, how we work and operate

Act in a professional manner that represents MAS’ values, Code of Conduct and Confidentiality

Defining the project with the client

Ensuring that the client agrees to the purpose and desired outcome of any assignment by discussing the Request for Consulting Services submitted to MAS by them

Ensuring that the nature of the problem is defined within the broader context of the client agency to make sure the defined project is appropriate in relation to other issues, developments etc.

Ensuring that any necessary client resources are available to support work associated with the assignment.

Applying appropriate diagnostic and problem-solving skills to develop the proposed solution

Creating a work plan including a timeline and outputs, to be approved by the client

Consulting and Advising

Implementing the agreed upon work plan

Consulting, as appropriate and necessary, with Practice Leaders, other VCs, or MAS Executive Director

Submitting Project updates monthly

Closing the Project

Initiating the Project Close form and sending to Client Services Manager

Seeking the client’s input as to how their effectiveness has been affected by the results of the project. 

Actively seeking additional assignments for and with the client

Actively seeking referrals to new clients

Requesting a donation from the client, as appropriate

Other

Contributing to the support and development of other MAS VC’s

Ensuring that technical skills are appropriate for each assignment

Ensuring professional skills are developed and kept current by various means. In particular, VCs should seek out critical feedback and take necessary action

Attending MAS-wide meetings and Practice Meetings

Appendix D – MAS Code of Conduct

I acknowledge and agree to conduct myself in accordance with the following rules of conduct in my work associated with MAS. 

Conflict of Interest – A volunteer/contract worker may not use any project for the purpose of soliciting personal business or for any other personal consideration or for soliciting business on behalf of others. The volunteer/contract worker should bring any questions concerning possible conflicts of interest to the attention of the Executive Director.

If the client has charitable status, the VC may choose to donate the recovery to the client and receive a charitable donation receipt for income tax purposes.  In no case will MAS reimburse a VC for project expenses.

Appendix E - MAS Confidentiality Policy

Background

The purpose of this policy is to ensure that all individuals working on behalf of MAS, all volunteer consultants, all Internal Volunteers, MAS contract workers, and any Director acting in his/her capacity as a member of the Board of Directors of MAS, working with organizations to whom they are referred by MAS (“MAS Organizations”), understand and agree with the importance of protecting from unauthorized disclosure all confidential information made available to them by MAS and/or MAS Organizations to whom they are referred both during periods of volunteer engagement and after completion of such engagements. This policy is intended to safeguard the integrity and reputations of MAS and MAS Organizations.

Policy Statement

MAS seeks to secure and protect from unauthorized disclosure all information related to MAS including its organization, strategies, business plans and operation and the information made available to MAS by MAS Organizations to whom referrals are made of volunteer consultants.

Each volunteer consultant who has agreed to act in such capacity with a MAS Organization to whom he/she is referred following an assessment of a Request for Consulting Services, and any MAS contract worker or internal volunteer or Director who has access to MAS Organization Confidential Information, must agree to the terms of this Policy by signing the MAS Code of Conduct prior to doing any work for MAS requiring their need to access any MAS Organization information.

Confidential Information for the purposes of this policy shall include:

Technical data, research, products, financial information, plans or strategies, forecasts or forecast assumptions, business practices, operations, procedures or services or marketing, merchandising or pricing information whether in documentary or electronic form;

Personal information about any individual;

Proprietary information of a Client Organization;	or

Any information that is designated or labeled as “Confidential” by MAS or a Client Organization that is given to or made accessible to MAS or a volunteer consultant who has been referred to the Client Organization by MAS.

By signing the MAS Code of Conduct, the signatory agrees:

to use the same reasonable precautions they would use to protect the confidential and proprietary information of their own or their clients, if any, in order to maintain the secrecy and confidentiality of such information;

not to disclose any Confidential Information to a third party (except as required by law) without the prior written consent of the disclosing party;

not to use the Confidential Information for any purpose other than as required to carry out any assignments resulting from referrals by MAS which the volunteer consultant has agreed to with the MAS Organization that the latter has disclosed to the volunteer consultant or MAS;

only to disclose the Confidential Information to MAS or other volunteer consultants or internal volunteers or third parties which MAS or the MAS Organization, as the case may be, agrees have a need to know the Confidential Information and who have signed a MAS Code of Conduct as well; and

not to make copies of Confidential information except as may be required for the performance of assignments with MAS Organizations to whom the volunteer consultant has been referred, or in the case of MAS contract workers, internal volunteers or Directors, who have a need to know the Confidential Information of such MAS Organization. All copies will upon reproduction contain the same proprietary and confidential notices and legends, which appear on the original documents and information

The obligations of confidentiality set out in this Policy do not apply to information that:

must be disclosed by law, judicial or arbitration process or by government authorities provided that the party required to disclose first gives the other party reasonable notice of such law, order or process;

is disclosed by the receiving party with prior written consent of the disclosing party; or

is otherwise in the public domain through no fault of the receiving party.

The receiving party, upon request of the disclosing party, agrees to return all Confidential Information to the disclosing party or destroy such Confidential Information and copies, if any, as directed by the disclosing party.

If the receiving party discovers that it has lost any Confidential Information it shall immediately notify the disclosing party.

Appendix F - MAS Sexual Harassment, Anti-Oppression and Anti-Racism Policy

Background

MAS is committed to providing an environment that is inclusive, equitable and free of discrimination and harassment, in which all volunteers are treated with respect and dignity, are able to contribute fully, and have equal opportunities.

Policy

Under the Ontario Human Rights Code, every person has the right to freedom from harassment and discrimination. Harassment and discrimination will not be tolerated, condoned, or ignored at MAS. If a claim of harassment or discrimination is proven, disciplinary measures will be applied, up to and including termination of a volunteer’s relationship with MAS. 

MAS is committed to making its volunteers aware of its anti-harassment and anti- discrimination policies. 

The objectives of this Policy are to:

Ensure that volunteers are aware that harassment and discrimination are unacceptable practices and are incompatible with the standards of the organization, as well as being a violation of the law.

Set out the types of behaviour that may be considered offensive and are prohibited by this Policy.

The right to freedom from discrimination and harassment extends to all volunteers, casual helpers and contract staff.

It is also unacceptable for any of the above to engage in harassment or discrimination when dealing with MAS clients, or with others with whom they have professional dealings, such as suppliers or service providers. Behaviours prohibited by this policy include discrimination, harassment, sexual harassment, sexual solicitation and poisoning of the environment. 

This Policy applies to every aspect of the workplace environment including volunteer recruitment and selection. This Policy also applies to events that occur with MAS clients.

This Policy prohibits discrimination or harassment on the basis of the following grounds, and any combination of these grounds:

Age

Creed (religion)

Sex (including pregnancy and breastfeeding)

Gender Identity

Family status (such as being in a parent-child relationship)

Marital status (including the status of being married, single, widowed, divorced, separated, or living in a conjugal relationship outside of marriage, whether in a same sex or opposite sex relationship)

Disability (including mental, physical, developmental or learning disabilities)

Race

Ancestry

Place of origin

Ethnic origin

Citizenship

Colour

Record of offences includes provincial offences such as under the Highway Traffic Act, and federal offences for which a pardon has been granted. 

All persons present in MAS are expected to uphold and abide by this Policy, by refraining from any form of harassment or discrimination, and by cooperating fully in any investigation of a harassment or discrimination complaint.

MAS leadership have the additional responsibility to act immediately on complaints or observations of harassment or discrimination. All complaints will be dealt with in a confidential manner respecting all parties involved. Leaders are responsible for creating and maintaining a harassment and discrimination-free organization, and should address potential problems before they become serious.
