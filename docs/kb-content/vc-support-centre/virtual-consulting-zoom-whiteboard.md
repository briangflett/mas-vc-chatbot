# Virtual Consulting - Using Zoom Whiteboard

Source: VC Support Centre (Google Drive)

MAS Strategic Planning

Using the new Zoom Whiteboard to facilitate Zoom brainstorming sessions

As a MAS Strategic Planning consultant, I struggled early in the pandemic (as did we all) to transfer the brainstorming techniques learned and honed in live sessions with client boards and management to something we could use in a virtual session. Developing a solid strategic plan always had involved getting everyone in a room, soliciting their ideas about the future and working as a team to determine the key themes identified, leading to the strategies and finally a strategic plan. 

Live Brainstorming

When the pandemic took hold and we all realized that we needed to find a solution that did not involve being in a room together, we all scrambled to find out what tools were out there and how best to make use of them. Now, despite the pandemic having passed for the most part, many clients have decided that the convenience of Zoom is something they would like to continue using. Those who live in the GTA love not having to drive in rush-hour traffic to join a board meeting. Organizations that are national appreciate involving all members of the team instead of just those that are local or that can afford to fly in for a brainstorming session. 

Tools like MIRO or the older version of the Zoom Whiteboard (with little to no functionality) seemed to hamper the experience of brainstorming on Zoom rather than enhance it. Instead, Zoom meetings were held with the working groups, ideas were discussed and shared, some amount of grouping of ideas and working on key themes was done in breakout sessions, eventually leading to an end product. 

The NEW Zoom Whiteboard

Features released by Zoom late in 2022 provide a much more user-friendly, and simple to use whiteboard functionality. The templates provided are very simple to copy and use. The setup of groupings and easily-updated and moveable yellow stickies is simple to use live with clients or preparing a whiteboard prior to a Zoom call.

Example of Brainstorming technique

After a bit of trial and error what I have found to be most useful for myself and my clients it to have them do some “homework” or pre-work prior to the Zoom session, sending me information which I then use to pre-populate the brainstorming whiteboard. Think of this as replacing the time in the live session where you ask each client for 5 ideas about, for instance, what the ideal ABC organization should look like 5 years from now and they fill out their 5 ideas on 5 yellow stickies. This visioning session is usually the first team brainstorming session I do with clients. 

The members of the strategic planning working team are asked to email me their “5 yellow stickies” containing their 5 ideas of what the org should ideally look like 5 years from now. After receiving an email from all team members with bullet-point suggestions, I then open a Zoom Whiteboard. This doesn’t need to be done in a live Zoom session and, in fact, works best if done prior to a live session. I fill in the whiteboard with the ideas sent by the team. I take a first pass at grouping the stickies into themes or groupings to come up with a draft whiteboard. Think of this as replacing the time in a live session where you, the facilitator, sometimes with help from a working team member, put the yellow-stickies on the board and attempt to group them into themes.

To do this, I open the Zoom Whiteboard, copy the simplest template, cut and paste to add groupings and stickies, fill in the wording in each yellow-sticky and group the stickies into my themes or groupings. You can then use the Export option in the menu to export the draft Whiteboard to PDF.

Live Zoom Brainstorming session

Now that you’ve collected the team’s ideas and drafted the first cut of the whiteboard, it’s time for a live Zoom session. The members should be coming to the Zoom having reviewed the draft whiteboard, preparing any questions or suggestions they might have for updating and improving it. Once the live session begins I usually start by summarizing the steps we’ve taken so far in our strategic planning efforts be they surveys, focus groups, environmental scans etc. Then I remind them that the process we are taking replaces the steps we would usually have done together in a room in a live brainstorming session (collecting their ideas on yellow stickies, posting and grouping them on the wall) and we are now ready to review what we’ve created and see how we might change it, update, add, reword, and move things around.

Typically, I work through the Whiteboard one grouping at a time, reading out the yellow-stickies for that particular theme and then asking if anyone has any questions about the content.  Some team members will want clarification about certain yellow-stickies, others can provide their input. Once questions are answered we move to the next grouping. Once all groupings are completed, I ask if there are any key things we feel are missing. Members will suggest ideas and I create a new yellow-stickie by cutting and pasting (right click) any of the yellow-stickies and changing the wording the what is suggested by the team member, moving it around as needed. Finally, if there is debate about a stickie fitting better in one grouping than another, the stickies can be moved around. 

What you end up with is very similar to what you would have up on the wall in a live session. In a live session I used to take a picture of what was on the wall with my iPad, facilitating the documentation of the session after the fact as well as providing photos of the team’s work which I shared with the team in the post-brainstorming write-up. 

You can now proceed as you would in a live session. I like to ask if these things are doable and have impact, using the concept of the do-ability/impact grid (Highly doable and high impact in top right, hard to do and low impact in bottom left). You can do that as a grid using a separate whiteboard or just talk it through taking notes. Using whatever techniques you like to close it out, you now have completed a productive brainstorming session where all members feel like they’ve had their “say” and have saved a lot of time (rather than having to let each member speak on the Zoom) of the typical brainstorming Zoom session.
