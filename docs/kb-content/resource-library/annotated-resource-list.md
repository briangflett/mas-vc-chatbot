MAS Annotated Resource List for Volunteer Consultants

June 2023

Over the years volunteers working with MAS have compiled an excellent set of on-line tools, articles and other resources in our MAS Resource Library.  They have been organized mostly along the Practice areas of MAS: governance, strategic planning, finance, fundraising etc.

Because by this point there are so many resources and because a quick look into the many Libraries and Folders doesn’t really tell you what’s in there without having to open each item, we have decided to put together this listing of titles with VERY brief descriptions.  

Although VCs are organized into Practice areas you may find helpful resources for your various projects in other areas; we’d recommend you scan this whole listing as there may be resources in different Libraries that can help you (or your client).

What’s great about the MAS Resource Library is that our VCs have done an amazing job of curating a wide range of items.  In future if and when you discover helpful tools, information, articles etc. it would be great if you could send us a copy so we can upload it. This slightly veers away from the previous process where any VC could insert a document directly.  

Moving forward we will continue to track new resources as they come in and maintain this annotated list.  The list itself will be kept on the VC page of the website and of course be available to send out on request.  We also often get requests from people needing various kinds of help or  information about not-for-profit life.  Now being able to put our hand quickly onto a resource we can share will be invaluable to our ability to respond.

If you have any comments or questions please don’t hesitate to contact me, or Nina (Client Services Manager).

Articles and Papers

7 Ideas to Build a Healthy Board Nomination Process

Helpful resource to share with ED’s and Boards

360-degree Governance and Where are the Directors in a World in Crisis

A 55-page examination from a practical and scholarly standpoint what good governance in the 21st century should be.  Produced by the Rotman School and written by Peter Dey and Susan Kaplan and with input from a huge Advisory Committee of Canadian corporate Who’s Who.  It’s from a very corporate perspective so lots is not applicable to NFP’s, however the 13 principals outlined in the 360-degree governance model would be very interesting for any Board – including not-for-profit, to adopt.

2017 Social and Demographic Trends by Jim Russell

An interesting short (3 page) discussion that explores a variety of issues and poses a number of questions for NFP Leaders such as “Despite your best efforts to master and deal with the new world of social media, how else might your business model be challenged in the era of the disappearance of context and attention spans?”.  Could form the basis of an interesting Board discussion session.

Imagine Canada’s Standards Program for Canada’s Charities and Nonprofits

This resource touches on all areas of MAS work and provides the baseline standard of practice in five areas: A. board governance B. financial accountability & transparency C. fundraising D. staff management and E. volunteer involvement.  The Standards Program is first and foremost a capacity-building initiative and Imagine Canada encourage organizations to use the standards to improve and monitor their practices.  They also run a vigorous accreditation program based on the Standards but it is neither required or in fact used very much as there is no widespread expectation among funders, the public etc. that NFP’s should be accredited.

CCA (Canadian Centre for Accreditation) Standards Manual for Community- Based Primary Health Care Organizations, Edition Date: May 11, 2020

Another accreditation guide, the standards outlined in this manual have been grouped especially for the Community-Based Primary Health Care organizations. The Organizational Standards in Section 2 apply to all organizations. Section 3 is sector specific, such as community-based primary care, child and youth mental health or credit counselling. Contact CCA to ensure you are using the correct manual for your sector.

Peering into the Future: Reimagining Governance in the Non-Profit Sector, BY LISA LALANDE, funded by Ignite NPS.

This paper focuses on how non-profit organizations can adapt to an increasingly complex environment by reimagining governance. It addresses how boards, leaders, staff and key stakeholders can work together to contribute to effective organizational governance from a strategic perspective. Specifically, this paper will: » Explore the current state of governance in the sector, including the legislative, regulatory and policy environment. » Identify emerging trends and challenges that impact governance at both the organizational and sector level. » Discuss promising practices and key considerations to improve the current state of governance. » Provide recommendations to improve the sector’s capacity for “future-oriented governance.”

Slide presentation: “Beyond Boards: Reimagining Governance, Disrupting the Status Quo” by Erin Kang - Ontario Nonprofit Network and Linda Mollenhauer - Ignite NPS

Covers: Overview of the Reimagining Governance Initiative and results of some of their consultations.  The Reimagining Governance, convened by the Ontario Nonprofit Network in partnership with Ignite NPS who produced the paper (above).  Reimagining Governance is an initiative designed to spark dialogue and new approaches to the way that nonprofit organizations are governed.  This slide presentation provides a glimpse of some interesting alternative views on NFP governance but wouldn’t likely be something MAS VCs would use with clients – at this point.

Restoring Trust in Nonprofits, ONN, 2019

Board Essentials, produced by the Ontario Nonprofit Network, 

This is an absolutely key document for general reference for any VC working with a Board.  A quick, 7-page, summary of the key responsibilities of a Board.  Could be used as a handout as well. 

“Board Essentials describes the requirements and responsibilities that must be fulfilled by the

board of directors, and which no other person or entity can perform. This core work of the board is based on what the law and regulations state are the requirements and responsibilities they must meet. While the board might play a significant role in fulfilling other governance functions, these are the essential ones.”  

Policy Statements (example) based on the Carver Board Governance Model

Produced by John Howard Society in 2014.  This is a helpful tool just to give you and idea of what a Board policy document, using the Carver Policy governance model, could look like. Voices of Board Chairs: A National Study on the Perspectives of Nonprofit Board Chairs, By Judy Freiwirth, Mary Hiland, Michael Burns, Gayle Gifford and Debra Beck | October 12, 2016

Very interesting discussion paper (17 pages).  An American perspective although that likely doesn’t change the perspective dramatically.  From the authors:  “As a group of practitioner researchers, we decided to find out what preparation is done by board chairs and how they see their role in relationship to the board and other stakeholders. What we found was a pretty glaring picture of neglect, in that this is an area of organizational leadership succession that is often insufficiently thought through. What follows are our findings from a survey of 635 self-identified nonprofit board chairs representing local, regional, and national organizations in forty-two states, and the recommendations to nonprofits we make in light of these findings.”
