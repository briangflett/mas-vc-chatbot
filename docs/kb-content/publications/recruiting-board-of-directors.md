Step One: Advance Planning

Start by defining your needs
Finalize a role description
Clear expectations of the role
Develop a board application form
Identify the volunteers who will help with recruitment and the steps they will be involved with
Develop a recruitment package (mission, history, description, list of current board members, executive summary of your strategic plan, etc.)

Nb: consider maintaining a list of potential board candidates and their key skills moving forward



































Step Two: Make your organization more visible in your community

Your organization’s work in the community must be valued and generally understood beyond the present board and staff group to attract volunteers.  Ideas to make your organization more visible include:
Make presentations about your organization 
Ensure your website promotes the importance of what your organization does and the difference it makes in people’s lives.  
Use social media and e-newsletters to communicate externally what the board is currently working on 
Partner with like-minded businesses (they too have enews, websites, social platforms, etc.!)




Can you leverage your Media Relations?

1. Make a list of local media outlets and possibilities.Study all of the newspapers, even the free ones, in your area. Listen to radio stations and find out who does interviews on talk shows.
2. Get to know the interviewing style and interests of local reporters.Listen to talk radio and read the community newspaper with an eye to analyzing the slant and area of interest of various reporters. The more familiar you become with their work, the easier it will be to find a “hook” that will interest them in what your organization is doing.
3. Keep looking for opportunities that are appropriate to you.One day I noticed a tiny paragraph in the business section of our newspaper which said, “Have you left corporate life to start something on your own? If so, we’d like to hear from you for an upcoming story we’re doing about career changers.”  I promptly called the reporter’s voice mail, introduced myself and noted insights from those who move into the NFP sector.
Another of my favorite tools is a letter to the editor. If you see a story that deals with an area related to your business, your professional comments may be welcomed. You could write to applaud the original article or add additional information.

Are there opportunities during upcoming media interviews to make a note of the need to volunteers?
Treat media interviews like donor prospecting -  they can be seed planting expeditions and good will come from every effort sooner or later.
Consider – Why are we newsworthy?
Consider the subject-matter-expertise your team has and what types of interviews could provide positive media opportunities for your organization.
What impact has your organization had in the community recently?  What human interest stories would local readers be interested in?  What public service / educational opportunities exist?   What are the benefits of volunteering your volunteers receive?

Step Three: Getting the word out to find qualified candidates

Post the  board member job descriptions on the website.  Make sure the annual report is also easy to find (many prospects will check out your website before considering).
Leverage relationships – schedule conversations with funders and other stakeholders to make the opportunities more visible and to ask for leads.
Create a social media post that your network of stakeholders can use to promote the opportunity to those within their network they feel might be a good fit




Social Media Post

Provide staff and volunteers with a post they can adapt or post your own and ask them to share
Post example

MASadvise CEO – the search is on!
If you are passionate about helping local non-profits create lasting positive impact please take a look at this Board of Directors posting.  It’s a great team and mission (HYPERLINK) #notforprofit

Share example add on
Attention (skill set needed e.g. accountants!) My colleague has let me know about this wonderful opportunity at MASadvise.  Check it out. #leadership #volunteering

By adding an image to the website page along with your job posting you will make sharing both the image and link very easy in social media channels.

Local advertising

Consider all the other places you could place this local ad once create
Local library – does it have a community bulletin board?
Local schools, YMCA / YWCAs, community centres, businesses, etc.

Rather than using traditional job / classified ads, consider opportunities to combine a program / service ad or another advertising need with a volunteer ad – it may be more visible to readers (better placement within the newspaper) and that increased visibility can create more awareness of the mission, program / service openings as well as the need for volunteers. 

If you advertising multiple times over the year, ensure you negotiate a more favourable rate based upon an annual contract vs. one-time rate.
